# Harbor field notes

A small, fictional plan for a quieter waterfront.

> **North star** — Make the harbor easy to explore, with room to pause.

## A thoughtful first visit

Begin at the old lighthouse. Follow the shoreline path to the reading room, then stop at the garden for a cup of tea. This plan is a public demonstration document; every place and project is fictional.

## Notes for the field guide

Keep sentences short, directions clear, and the pace unhurried. A useful guide should help visitors find their own way—not tell them what to enjoy.

## The shoreline path

The route follows the water before turning into a sheltered lane. Leave a little extra time here: the best part of a walk is often the pause you did not plan.

### Wayfinding

Use familiar words on the signs. Give each turn a clear landmark. Keep the map legible at a glance.

### A place to pause

Put a bench near the garden gate. A view of the water and a little shade are more useful than another instruction.

## Reading room

The reading room is a quiet landing place. A shelf of local stories, a large table, and good light are all it needs.

### A welcoming shelf

Collect short essays, illustrated maps, and notebooks with room for visitors to add a thought.

### Shared notes

Leave a pencil beside the guest book. Invite a sentence about something noticed along the way.

## Harbor garden

Plant for the seasons, not just opening day. Make room for herbs, flowering borders, and a sheltered table.

### Tea at the garden

Offer one simple blend and fresh water. Let the conversation set its own pace.

### Closing the loop

The return path leads past the reading room and back to the lighthouse. End where you began, with a different view of the harbor.
